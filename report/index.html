InceptionV1 · mixed4e:55 and mixed4d:493 · two pre-registered tests

Is the cat-and-car neuron a V detector?

The essay Neural Networks Are Already Clean (Goodfire, 2026) reinterprets InceptionV1's best-known polysemantic neuron, 4e:55, as one detector of V shapes: pointed things drive it, round things push it negative. That reading rests on co-occurrence: which images activate the unit, and which units it connects to. We tested it by changing shape and content independently, on the unit itself and on its largest input, 4d:493, which the essay calls the "fox snout" channel.

Run 17 Oct 2026 on an Apple M1 Max · ImageNetV2, Oxford-IIIT Pets, DTD · each test hashed before it ran

The claim, and why top images can't settle it

Earlier interpretability work described 4e:55 as responding to cat faces and car fronts, and treated it as the textbook polysemantic neuron. The essay argues it is really a V-shape detector. Its evidence: the unit's top images, its strongest inputs and readers (flames, coyote faces, martini glasses), and photos where pointed objects drive it while round ones push it negative.

In natural photos, shape and category travel together. The essay's positive examples are dogs, foxes, cats and cars; its negative ones are golf balls, clocks and snails. A unit that responds to cat faces and car fronts would produce the same top-image grid as a unit that responds to V shapes. Telling them apart takes stimuli where shape changes and content doesn't, and the reverse.

Setup

  • Model. InceptionV1 with the original Lucid weights (inception5h, loaded through Lucent). 4e:55 is channel 55 of mixed4e's 1×1 branch. 4d:493 is channel 29 of mixed4d's pool-projection branch, a 1×1 convolution over a 3×3 max-pool of mixed4c.
  • Same unit as the essay. Over 4e:55's top 200 images, its largest input is 4d:493 at 34% of positive drive (the essay reports 32%), and its strongest 1×1 reader is 5a:94 (the essay's "flames" reader).
  • Readout. Post-ReLU spatial maximum over the 14×14 map, divided by the mean of the unit's top 0.2% of ImageNetV2 images (Aref = 1257 for 4e:55, 2657 for 4d:493). So 1.0 is a typical top natural image. The pre-registered threshold for "strong" is 0.30.
  • Data. ImageNetV2 matched-frequency (10,000 images, 10 per class, standing in for ImageNet validation, which needs an account). Oxford-IIIT Pets (7,349 images with segmentation trimaps; head boxes for 3,686). DTD textures as neutral fills.
  • Validity checks. 4d:493's top 50 images include 49 mammals. Some synthetic stimulus drives 98% of mixed4e channels and 97.5% of mixed4d channels to ≥ 0.30, and known curve detectors reach a median of 0.78 on drawn arcs. The synthetic stimuli are not out of distribution for these layers.
  • Tuning, frozen before testing. Each unit's best V over 4 apex angles × 24 orientations × 3 sizes. 4e:55: 120° apex, θ = 345°, 160 px (a wide wedge, apex down). 4d:493: 30° apex, θ = 90°, 112 px (a narrow wedge pointing left, like a snout in profile).
  • Exact routes. Both units are 1×1 convolutions over the layer below. Each activation therefore splits exactly into per-channel input contributions (reconstruction error under 0.01%), which lets us ask whether cats, vehicles and synthetic shapes arrive through the same inputs.
  • Statistics. Means with 95% bootstrap intervals (10,000 resamples). "≈" means the 90% interval lies within ±0.10.
  • Procedure. Each pre-registration was hashed (SHA-256) before its test. Pipelines were debugged on a neighboring unit (4e:54, 4d:492). Every reported result is the first run on the real unit.

1 · Shape alone barely drives either unit

Textured silhouettes on gray, with no category content, reach only a small fraction of what natural images do. For 4e:55, a sharp apex adds nothing: the V (0.12) and rounded U (0.11) tie, V − U = +0.01 [−0.01, 0.04]. In a separate exploratory sample, a corner-free ellipse did as well as the V (0.125 vs 0.113). What 4e:55 prefers is an elongated outline over a compact disk.

4d:493 is the one place in these tests where pointedness shows up. Its V beats the rounded U by +0.05 [0.03, 0.07] and the ellipse by +0.04 [0.02, 0.06]. Both gaps are real but small, and its best shape still reaches only 0.096.

Adding a pair of eyes to a disk changed nothing for either unit (4e:55 +0.001, 4d:493 +0.002), so the face-layout hypothesis gets no support either.

2 · The interior carries the drive, not the outline

We took each unit's top 200 natural images (100 ImageNetV2, 100 Pets) and kept the object's exact outline while flattening or scrambling everything inside it. The essay's account predicts most of the response survives. It doesn't. Mean retained drive: 4e:55 0.15 (flat) and 0.11 (scrambled); 4d:493 0.10 and 0.08. Medians are lower still (0.11, 0.08; 0.06, 0.03). Editing the background instead leaves 97–99%.

3 · Apertures: the V's advantage shrinks as more of the face shows

An aperture test shows the same content through windows of different shape but equal area. Study 1's pre-registered version used windows that covered only about 18% of the face. There the V beat the disk by +0.14 on cat faces, a shape share of 0.70 [0.65, 0.75]. For the unit's top vehicle images the shape share was 0.42.

In an exploratory rerun with windows four times larger (74% of the face), the disk overtook the V on cat faces (0.46 vs 0.39), and on the top vehicle images the two tied (0.53 vs 0.53). It was run after seeing the pre-registered result, so it is not part of the verdict.

4 · Input routes: cats and cars arrive by different paths

For each image group we took the unit's input contributions at its peak location, and kept the smallest set of input channels carrying half the positive drive. For 4e:55, the cat and vehicle routes share a single channel, 4d:493. The overlap, relative to the split-half ceiling, is 0.11. Because 4d:493 sits in both, the vehicle route still carries 0.82× as much of the cat drive as the cat route does. In the other direction, the cat route carries only 0.37× as much of the vehicle drive as the vehicle route.

That pattern looks like two category routes joined by one shared input, not a single shape mechanism. For 4d:493 itself, cats, dogs and foxes share many inputs (Jaccard overlap with the cat route 0.48–0.59), vehicles fewer (0.21). Too few V silhouettes drove it (n = 26) to run the shape half of the route test.

◆ marks channels shared by the two routes in each pair. The 4d:493 routes need 20 (cats) and 23 (dogs) channels to reach half the drive; the first 8 are listed. Labels are each input channel's three commonest classes among its top 30 ImageNetV2 images: a description, not an explanation.

5 · Natural images: pointed faces aren't preferred

Grouping breeds by breed-standard head shape, chosen before measurement: for cats, round-faced breeds drive both units slightly more than pointed ones. The pointed-minus-round differences are −0.07 [−0.11, −0.02] for 4e:55 and −0.04 [−0.08, −0.01] for 4d:493. The Persian cat is 4e:55's #2 ImageNet class, and on ImageNetV2, Siamese minus Persian is −0.37 [−0.58, −0.16].

Dogs point the other way: pointed-minus-round is +0.09 [0.06, 0.12] and +0.14 [0.11, 0.17]. But the flat-faced Japanese chin is the top dog breed for both units, while the pointed, short-coated miniature pinscher scores low. Coat and face type predict the response better than pointedness.

Pointed objects chosen in advance (guitar picks, missiles, speedboats, spires, volcanoes) average 0.17 for 4e:55, against 0.14 for round objects and a class median of 0.14. The essay's own examples rank #32 (pool table), #43 (bullet train) and #489 (fireboat) of 1,000 classes for 4e:55. For 4d:493 they rank #275, #181 and #239.

What this does and doesn't show

When shape and content are varied separately, the V account explains a small part of what drives 4e:55. Most of the drive comes from object interiors (faces, fur, car grilles), arriving through partly separate cat and vehicle inputs. Shape adds a modest, apex-insensitive preference for elongated outlines. The essay's proposed source of the V, the "fox snout" input 4d:493, is itself driven mainly by furry mammal faces. It has a small sharp-apex preference, an order of magnitude below its content effect.

None of this says the V is a useless description. It may be a helpful summary of what these units' top images share. It also doesn't touch the essay's broader claims about recursive generalization or superposition. It does show that the canonical example was reinterpreted from co-occurrence evidence. Under direct manipulation, support for that reinterpretation is weak: mixed by our pre-registered rules for 4e:55, and refuted for 4d:493, the input the essay names as its source.

Limitations.

  • Two units in one model.
  • All edits are automatic: masks, windows, phase scrambles.
  • Equal-area windows reveal different parts of the content.
  • "V" was operationalized as a straight-edged wedge; the essay never defines it precisely.
  • ImageNetV2 has 10 images per class, and there are only 16 vehicle images in 4e:55's top 100.
  • Both pre-registrations were written by the same analyst who ran the tests. The hashes fix their timing, not their independence.

Deviations, all logged before the affected test ran.

  • Study 1: an identity check missed one sub-criterion (2 vehicles in the top 50, not 3). Two indexing checks against the essay's own figure confirmed it is the same unit.
  • Study 1: the shape-share formula was corrected so negative effects count as zero.
  • Study 2: the ellipse matches the V's long axis rather than its base.
  • Study 2: the rounded-V construction from study 1 is malformed at 30°, so it was rebuilt to be valid at any angle.
